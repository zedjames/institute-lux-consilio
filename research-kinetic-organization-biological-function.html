Kinetic Organization and Biological Function

How organized movement and coupled physical dynamics produce specified biological functions.

Guiding question

How can coupled kinetic mechanisms sharing finite matter and energy sustain a selected function, and what can partial observation establish about that operation?

Program

Research trajectory

Paper I develops a momentum-bearing mechanochemical transporter under prescribed chemical and ionic boundaries. Its hybrid Langevin–jump generator separates hidden mechanical state from recorded phase and ionic transport histories, with function-specific inference and finite-window certification.

Paper II lets active ion transport, passive return and external regeneration act on the same finite ionic and chemical inventories and an inventory-derived membrane capacitor. Every event changes the conditions and feasible rates of later events. Exact conservation, local detailed balance and separate free-energy and caloric accounts specify the complete coupled source.

For the actual nonzero-coupling kinetic model, the second paper establishes unique invariant laws. Closed chemistry has a reversible Gibbs equilibrium with zero signed stationary currents, while maintained regeneration sustains strictly positive pump–leak circulation and entropy production despite zero net mean charge accumulation. Certified intervals quantify charge-band occupancy, exit flux and two stationary survival horizons; a passive-assisted theorem controls recovery from extreme charge.

These selected functionals differ from permanent confinement: almost every trajectory eventually leaves the designated charge band. The band and all energetic parameters belong to a synthetic, not experimentally calibrated, model. The distinction between qìjī (organized movement) and qìhuà (transformation) supplies historical motivation rather than a physical identity claim about qi.

Methods

  • Hybrid momentum-bearing Langevin–jump transport and shared guarded finite inventories
  • Local detailed balance, pathwise conservation, electrical/capacitive and caloric accounting
  • Kinetic Lyapunov bounds, accessible graph minorization and Harris invariant laws
  • Path-reversal entropy production and stationary pump–leak current identities
  • Outward interval rate envelopes, killed survival recursions and bounded stationary correctors

From Kinetic Movement to Biological Function

Functional descent, historical energy, and certified observation in coupled transport processes

A synthetic reversible four-phase, single-ion mechanochemical transporter connects continuous mechanical motion to conformational jumps, chemical reservoirs, and signed ionic release. Equal observed phase and kinetic energy need not determine future function: at nonzero coupling two mechanical preparations have initial outside-release rates differing by a factor of approximately 1.35. Conditioning on transition histories yields a measure-valued predictive state. For specified finite windows, source-derived backward bounds certify signed transport and outside-edge event probability. Exact caloric path identities link endpoint phase, signed ion transport, and energy accounting. Results concern a specified synthetic source rather than a calibrated protein or a general physical definition of qi.

Coupled Kinetic Processes and the Maintenance of Biological Function

Shared finite reservoirs, sustained nonequilibrium circulation, and selected charge-band persistence

Extending Paper I from prescribed boundaries to dynamically shared resources, this study constructs a momentum-bearing four-phase ion transporter coupled to passive return, finite ionic and chemical inventories, and an inventory-derived membrane capacitor. Exact pathwise conservation and separate caloric/free-energy accounts support a complete hybrid generator with unique invariant kinetic laws. Closed chemistry gives reversible Gibbs equilibrium; maintained regeneration produces strictly positive pump–leak circulation and entropy production even with zero mean charge drift. Source-wide bounds distinguish stationary charge-band occupancy, exit flux, uninterrupted survival and extreme-charge recovery. These results describe a synthetic, explicitly normalized source rather than a calibrated physiological homeostasis threshold.

Current frontier

What the public record hands forward.

Papers I–II establish function-specific observation, exact coupled finite-resource kinetics, unique invariant laws and sustained nonequilibrium circulation for a declared synthetic source. The next questions concern multiple interacting transporters, the sufficiency of experimentally accessible observations, replacement of synthetic labels with calibrated physical mechanisms, and extension of the preservation/recovery criteria without assuming indefinite confinement.

Open questions

  1. 01

    How do shared finite reservoirs organize multiple transporters and collective chemical/ionic regulation?

  2. 02

    Which measurements distinguish active circulation and energetic consumption when mean membrane charge drift vanishes?

  3. 03

    Which function-specific occupancy, survival, exit and recovery properties persist under measured protein-specific physical parameters?

  4. 04

    Can experimental observations validate the proposed mechanism-resolved functional and thermodynamic relationships?
