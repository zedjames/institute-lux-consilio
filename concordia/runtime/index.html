Concordia · Research environment

Concordia Runtime

The developing professional interface between the Formal World and the researchers and institutions who investigate it.

Currently in Alpha Testing · Closed beta targeted for Q2 2027

An inhabited digital scientific laboratory: governed projections of formal knowledge, persistent research inhabitants, source-aware inquiry, and inspectable evidence.

Purpose

The Formal World, open to governed inquiry

Concordia Runtime is being developed as the Institute’s professional interface to its Formal World. It presents authorized, versioned projections of an evolving mathematical and computational corpus as a navigable scientific research environment. Researchers and institutions can engage specific constructions, questions, dependencies and records through that interface, under the applicable permissions.

The Formal World is an extensive and continuously growing source corpus. Cloning, storing and building the entire repository is not a practical default requirement for scholars who wish to examine a particular claim. Runtime is designed to bring relevant, bounded parts of that world into view, with their source identities, provenance, coverage, and evidentiary boundaries intact.

The environment supports continuing research across changing questions, sources and cognitive tools. Models contribute reasoning and expression as replaceable faculties; they do not own the corpus or establish the authority of their own outputs. Concordia governs scientific identity, authorized access, durable research history and consequential actions.

Core principle

Cognition and scientific authority remain distinct

A hypothesis or model-generated conclusion may guide investigation. A formal statement, computational certificate or empirical finding retains its own hypotheses, verification boundary and evidentiary standing.

A projected scientific laboratory

Worlds made accessible through inhabitants

Concordia is conceived as an inhabited research environment. Registered Worlds are structured dimensions of the scientific corpus, and their authorized projections form working spaces for investigation. Persistent inhabitants act as the environment’s digital scientific staff: they orient visiting researchers, introduce relevant concepts, navigate connections, support inquiry and help examine supporting records. Their roles, permissions and retained histories are governed by the institution.

The Formal World

The evolving source of mathematical declarations, constructions, dependencies and research history. Its scale and continuity exceed what an individual paper or local checkout can conveniently convey.

Runtime projection

A governed view of identified portions of that world: typed objects, source locations, relations, verification records and authorized tools. A projection identifies what is covered and what remains outside the present view.

Research inhabitants

Persistent AI research participants assigned to particular Worlds and mandates. They serve as guides and scientific interlocutors within the laboratory, with source-aware assistance and continuity across sessions.

“Digital scientific staff” describes the intended role of governed AI inhabitants within Runtime; it does not imply human employment, independent scientific authority or that all envisioned inhabitants and Worlds are already available to external researchers.

Verification and access

Evidence without a full-corpus checkout

As the Formal World grows, a full local fork or reconstruction of the repository cannot be the ordinary entry point for scholarly examination. Runtime is being developed to support claim-scoped, source-bound review: identify the precise declaration or construction, traverse its relevant dependencies, examine original sources and assumptions, and inspect the checks and records that support the reported result.

Such examination must distinguish a formal statement from its physical interpretation, and a machine-checked derivation from independent reproducibility. A Runtime view can facilitate this work without itself proving that every statement in the entire corpus has been checked. Where a review requires rerunning a check or obtaining source materials, the relevant evidence and access must be arranged at an appropriate, authorized scope.

Independent scrutiny remains a scientific objective. Projections, verified provenance and governed access should reduce the cost of meaningful review, while preserving what was examined, what was unavailable and what remains unverified.

Scientific accountability

Examination is specific, attributable and bounded

A navigable projection or an inhabitant’s explanation is not a substitute for inspectable hypotheses, underlying source identity and appropriate checks. Verification evidence retains its toolchain, revision, scope and limitations.

These are developing interfaces and review workflows in Alpha Testing, not a claim that independent remote verification of every formal result is already available.

Research architecture

Worlds, inhabitants and continuity

Registered Worlds

Formal World indexes mathematical modules, declarations and relations. Source World contains versioned repositories, files, source ranges and diffs. The World Navigator supports bounded inspection, typed traversal and explicit coverage of the sources examined.

Inhabitants across Worlds

An inhabitant’s governed assignment and residence within a World can persist beyond a model session or conversation. Such research staff may guide discovery, introduce unfamiliar structures, connect questions and support examination; all participation, memory and actions remain permissioned.

Governed memory

Conversation history, proposed lessons, retained evidence and admitted developmental memory are distinct. An interesting model answer is not automatically made into authoritative or durable knowledge; admission requires its own authorized process.

Scholarly and professional participation

Research collaboration by arrangement

Concordia Runtime is intended as the primary professional point of contact between visiting researchers or institutions and the Institute’s evolving Formal World. Through qualified, governed participation, a collaborator can work with inhabitants and projected scientific evidence instead of beginning with a complete local repository copy. Any external access remains scoped to the deployed capabilities, authorized materials and applicable research agreement.

Scholarly examination

Review a paper's provenance, mathematical hypotheses, selected formal statements, computational records or source-specific verification evidence by arrangement.

Joint investigation

Pursue a defined scientific problem with source-aware inquiry, retained questions, explicit obstructions and distinguishable evidentiary outcomes.

Institutional collaboration

Structure research around agreed objectives, governance, confidentiality, relevant corpus access, resources, authorship, publication and intellectual property.

Qualification and scope

Implemented infrastructure, continuing development

Concordia Runtime includes implemented internal persistence, research Worlds, cognitive-provider governance, source navigation, formal inquiry and an integrated research interface. A first persistent inhabitant's Formal World residence received a separately authorized live adoption in October 2026; subsequent conversational-grounding work has passed Development checks, with live cognitive behavior and production qualification remaining separately assessed.

The original inhabitant was instantiated for a particular Fieldflux Biosystems research mandate. Its lineage is distinct from the ILC-owned institutional corpus and is not a universal template for external collaborators.

Current access boundary

Not a public self-service service

Concordia is not advertised as public SaaS, unrestricted repository access, autonomous scientific certification, clinical authority, external multi-inhabitant tenancy or unrestricted code execution. Specific collaborations require governance, authorization and technical qualification.
