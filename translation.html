Research & translation

Science can travel without losing its home.

ILC separates the continuity of a scientific program from the commercial structures that may translate parts of that program into products and services.

Foundational research

Fieldflux Biosystems

Commercial translation

A separately incorporated commercial company under ILC's institutional control. Product engineering, hardware, software, regulatory development, manufacturing, financing, and commercialization.

Scientific continuity

FFB owns and commercially develops its integrated technology estate. ILC retains permanent scientific-use rights sufficient to continue research, formal verification, scholarly writing, publication, preservation, and scientific collaboration around that work.

New research

Independent future ILC research belongs to ILC under the institute's governing framework. Where new ILC technology is relevant to FFB's business, FFB receives a structured first commercial opportunity rather than automatic ownership.

Clinical practice

Clinical practice remains a distinct domain. ILC's research agreements do not themselves transfer patient records, clinical authority, protected health information, or ownership of an independent clinical practice.

Why the separation matters

The structure lets foundational research remain publishable and scientifically continuous while commercial organizations carry product-specific risk, capital requirements, engineering obligations, and market execution. Neither side is reduced to the other.
