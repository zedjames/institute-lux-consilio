Methods

How do we learn what a question permits us to say?

Every method is a way of seeing. Mathematics reveals structure. Formal proof sharpens necessity. Computation explores possibility. Observation connects an idea to the physical world.

01

Mathematics

We use mathematical structure to ask what follows from a definition, which quantities remain invariant, and where a theory changes character.

02

Formal proof

Machine-assisted proof lets definitions, dependencies, assumptions, and theorems become inspectable scientific objects.

03

Computation

Computation lets us explore representations, build counterexamples, test sensitivities, reconstruct structures, and discover patterns worth explaining.

04

Observation

Empirical work connects abstract structure to biological and physical systems through calibration, measurement, comparison, and reproducible evidence.

Rigor is a form of curiosity.

We want to know what a result says, why it says it, which assumptions carry it, and how far its authority reaches. Formal verification, sensitivity analysis, negative results, controls, and reproducible computation help turn those questions into a transparent scientific record.

Different forms of evidence illuminate different layers.

A theorem can establish a mathematical proposition. A computation can expose a structure or test a construction. An experiment can connect a model to the world. A measurement can become meaningful when its provenance and calibration are understood. ILC keeps those layers visible so they can work together.

Formal scientific infrastructure

The institute maintains a large formal and computational research environment supporting work across health, biology, fluid dynamics, mathematical physics, and quantum gravity. Public verification artifacts are released as the research programs mature.
