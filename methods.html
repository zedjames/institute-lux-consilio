Foundational questions

Objects, relationships, and physical exchange.

What is a physical object?

We often begin by identifying a system, defining its properties, and then studying its interactions. ILC investigates whether this order of explanation conceals something important: the relationships and exchanges through which systems interact may help establish the properties by which those systems become distinguishable.

An object may be understood as a relatively stable organization of relationships and exchanges. Its boundaries describe conditions through which it remains identifiable, interacts with its surroundings, and changes.

A boundary may carry geometry or physical degrees of freedom, regulate material and energetic exchange, constrain information, or change through the very interactions it mediates. Mathematical domain boundaries, physical interfaces, changes of representation, and limits of continuation each require their own precise descriptions.

We investigate what remains identifiable through these relationships, what is conserved or transformed, and what conditions allow organized physical systems to persist. Across different research programs, those questions provide opportunities to discover whether common principles govern genuinely different systems and scales.

Beyond checking proofs

From verification to mathematics developed to interact.

Formal verification establishes that a theorem follows from its stated definitions and assumptions. Formal libraries preserve such results for reuse. ILC investigates a further scientific possibility: what becomes possible when mathematical constructions are intentionally developed to interact in continuing research?

Concordia maintains typed mathematical objects, proof dependencies, source histories, counterexamples, and qualified interfaces. A result inherited by another investigation must preserve its assumptions; new relationships require mathematical compatibility and appropriate physical justification.

Intentionally connecting existing structures can reveal new theorems, missing interfaces, or precise obstructions that neither investigation anticipated. These interactions are themselves part of the research.

Why research develops in series

Follow the question through successive constructions.

A mathematical or physical finding can open new questions about its assumptions, observables, boundary conditions, and relationship to other systems. ILC research series investigate these consequences rather than treating each publication as an isolated endpoint.

Each stage distinguishes what earlier work establishes, what the new construction inherits, which additional conditions it introduces, and where a proof succeeds or encounters an obstruction. A paper is a scientific contribution within its stated scope; the series preserves the larger continuing investigation.

This makes it possible to pursue a physical question across different mathematical demands without silently transferring a result beyond its legitimate domain.

Research for future interpreters

Preserve the source. Constrain interpretation. Enable inquiry.

A specialist reading a paper often supplies tacit disciplinary knowledge. An AI interlocutor may offer convincing explanations but cannot reliably identify which unstated assumptions or derivations belong to the original work. A plausible reconstruction is not necessarily an established result.

A research source should preserve what has been established, why it follows, and where its authority applies. Its definitions, assumptions, mathematical relationships, and evidence should be sufficiently explicit to constrain interpretation to what the research supports, while identifying the additional conditions or evidence required for further conclusions.

ILC manuscripts therefore record the relevant physical source, proof scope, computation, uncertainties, dependencies, and open questions. Where appropriate, Concordia identifies the associated mathematical records and the limits of their verification. Unresolved scientific questions invite further inquiry; missing source evidence must remain identifiable rather than silently filled in.
