Methods

Claims should carry their method with them.

ILC combines formal mathematics, analytical work, computation, empirical analysis, and explicit calibration. Different methods establish different kinds of authority.

Formal

Machine-assisted proof

Definitions, theorem dependencies, proof terms, executable checks, and machine-verifiable claims are used where formalization materially sharpens the scientific question.

Analytical

Mathematical derivation

Proofs and derivations are written so assumptions, domains, boundary conditions, and failure modes remain visible rather than hidden behind notation.

Computational

Reproducible experiments

Computational work records inputs, methods, sensitivity analyses, negative controls, and enough provenance to distinguish an observed output from an interpreted result.

Empirical

Calibration before claim

Empirical claims are bounded by the calibration, controls, data-generating process, and exact estimator that support them. Negative results are retained as evidence.

Formal scientific infrastructure

The institute maintains a substantial formal and computational research environment. Public artifacts are released when doing so supports verification and scholarly use; private implementation material can remain protected while the scientific result itself is published.

Scientific provenance

Material future work is attributed to an originating capacity—ILC Research, FFB Commercial, or a Joint Project—so authorship, ownership, and scientific provenance remain legible as the institutions grow.
