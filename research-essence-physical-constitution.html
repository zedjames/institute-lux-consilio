Result 01

Relational qualities develop through physical evolution

Real scalar field on a fixed Schwarzschild interior

Starting from a normalized one-excitation state with zero anomalous correlation, the action-derived Bogoliubov evolution generates a nonzero anomalous correlation at a physically realized later interior clock. Independently, matched even-parity preparations with equal occupation and norm but different anomalous correlations yield different later occupations.

A(t) = 3α(t)β(t); N(t) = 1 + 3|β(t)|²

The state can acquire a relational quality through evolution, and that quality can be consequential to a subsequent physical response. Generation and consequence are demonstrated on their respective controlled preparations.

Result 02

Continuity has an exact relational representation

Completed scalar Fock graph

The zero-mode quadratic moments N (occupation), A (anomalous correlation) and w (squared norm) obey a closed two-time evolution on the free-Hamiltonian domain, including non-Gaussian states. Their Gram determinant is conserved.

Δ = N(N + w) − |A|²; Δ(U(t,s)Ψ) = Δ(Ψ)

The individual moments may change while an exact constitutive relation persists. The reduced representation is sufficient for the declared quadratic responses; the full state carries further distinctions.

The foundational inquiry

The physical meaning of essence

Essence (精) motivates the investigation of the qualities constitutive of physical form and its possibilities of transformation. Paper I develops this inquiry through exact quantum and gravitational response laws. The historical expression 化生精，氣生形 (Transformation generates Essence; Qi generates form) motivates the study of qualities developed through physical transformation.

Essence is not simply the material from which a physical object is assembled. It is the elementary physical reality whose intrinsic and relational qualities make the constitution of physical form possible.

Essence, understood as a general constitutive principle, refers to the elementary physical constituents from which more complex physical organizations arise. These constituents possess essential properties belonging to their physical identity, while additional qualities may emerge through interactions, relationships, and exchanges.

Proposed physical constitution principle. Elementary physical constituents possess essential properties that determine their lawful possibilities of interaction. Through exchanges across physical boundaries, constituents develop additional relational qualities and participate in the constitution of larger organized forms. These forms acquire distinguishable properties and capacities that govern their subsequent interactions. Physical individuality and continuity arise through the persistence of such organizations within the interconnected dynamics of the larger physical system.

These statements provide the foundational direction of the program. Its scientific development proceeds through explicitly specified physical states, relational qualities, interaction laws and response observables, with the scope of each conclusion determined by its mathematical and physical source.

Five research hypotheses

What must be demonstrated?

Each hypothesis frames an ongoing investigation within declared physical theories. Paper I supplies model-specific contributions to these questions; further work investigates their reach, correspondences and obstructions.

HYPOTHESIS 01

Elementary Constitution and Essential Identity

Physical constituents possess fundamental characteristics that establish their elementary identity and determine their lawful possibilities of physical participation.

Research question. Determine which properties are common to elementary physical constituents, which distinguish different constituent types, and which depend upon their representation or physical state.

Candidate formal objects: Quantum representations, invariants, dynamical laws, and observable consequences.

HYPOTHESIS 02

Differentiated Essential Qualities and Constitutive Possibility

Elementary physical constituents possess distinguishable essential qualities whose differences influence their interactions and the physical organizations they can constitute.

Research question. Classify intrinsic, state-dependent and relationally generated qualities and determine how they change lawful interaction responses and subsequent physical possibilities.

Candidate formal objects: Mass, spin, charge, spectral composition, modes, phase relationships, couplings and their theory-dependent roles.

HYPOTHESIS 03

Boundary Exchange and Relational Physical Identity

The distinguishability and individuality of physical objects arise through structured boundaries of interaction and exchange within a larger connected physical system.

Research question. Establish when gradients, interfaces and exchange laws support effective independence, coupling, transmission, and persistent physical continuity.

Candidate formal objects: Boundary observables, fluxes, coupling conditions, exchange constraints and effective autonomy.

HYPOTHESIS 04

Relational Generation of Essential Qualities

Interactions among elementary physical constituents can generate additional essential qualities that become defining properties of the resulting physical organization.

Research question. Determine when coupled dynamics create new collective modes, correlations, conserved structures, response properties and stable configurations with constitutive significance.

Candidate formal objects: Coupled systems, collective observables, response kernels, conservation structures and dynamical stability.

HYPOTHESIS 05

Constitutive Continuity and the Development of Physical Form Across Scale

Physical organizations can acquire persistent constitutive identities through the continuing relationships and exchanges of their constituents, allowing organized forms to become effective constituents of larger physical systems.

Research question. Determine criteria for inheritance, dynamical autonomy, transformation and loss of organized identity through changing configuration, exchange and scale.

Candidate formal objects: Effective descriptions, multiscale mappings, invariant or stable organization, and counterexamples.

The first scientific realizations

Quantum excitation, composition and gravitational response

Paper I begins with a completed real-scalar Fock representation, a symmetric radiative two-particle Hilbert space and a source-matched Einstein–scalar family, each with specified dynamics and physical response.

The scalar results distinguish state continuation, generated anomalous correlations and a sufficient quadratic representation. The radiative results isolate constituent overlap and identify an exact response-history quotient. Subsequent studies may extend the question to interacting quantum fields, gravitating systems, kinetic organizations, and collective or continuum descriptions. Each step will require a justified map between the physical carriers and their observables; agreement of vocabulary is not a substitute for a mathematical bridge.

Scientific method

How constitutive claims are established

Our aim is to discover which claims can be made precise and established within actual mathematical and physical systems, which require stronger conditions, and where counterexamples reveal meaningful limitations.

Declare a physical carrier

Specify a theory, constituent type, state representation, observables and dynamical law before making a constitutive assertion.

Identify invariant and state-dependent qualities

Separate representation invariants, physically meaningful state properties and interaction-generated quantities. Audit which are gauge-invariant or operationally distinguishable.

Construct a lawful interaction

Specify actual coupling channels, boundary conditions, conservation constraints and the exchange maps that connect the constituents.

Test the resulting organization

Establish when collective observables, modes or response characteristics arise, whether they remain stable, and which initial differences affect the result.

Investigate inheritance and loss

Define mappings to larger-scale descriptions, test whether effective properties persist, and record cases where the proposed identity or continuity fails.

Compare theories and obstructions

Separate what holds only in the declared physical model from transferable structures, qualified bridges, empirical questions and explicit counterexamples.
