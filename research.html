Research atlas

Programs, not isolated papers.

The primary unit of ILC research is the scientific program: a durable question that accumulates papers, proofs, calculations, computational experiments, notes, failures, and subsequent directions.

Cross-cutting themes

Questions recur across fields.

ILC programs remain distinct, but several conceptual questions repeatedly connect them: identity through transformation, continuation, representation, evidence, relational organization, and the boundary between a mathematical object and a warranted observation.

Reading the archive

Each program has a history.

A paper can establish a result. A program records why that result mattered, what it depended upon, what failed, what became possible next, and where the boundary of the claim sits. ILC's archive preserves that history rather than flattening research into a feed.

Program pages now carry the paper sequence, guiding question, methods, current frontier, open questions, and public research artifacts. Publication records preserve the original DOI and historical provenance while ILC supplies the long-term institutional context.
