Scientific inheritance

An environment for questions not yet asked.

Scientific publications communicate discoveries, arguments and conclusions. Concordia extends this tradition through a living Formal World of mathematical objects, definitions, proofs, dependencies, research histories and established obstructions.

When the necessary compatibility is established, constructions from one research program can participate in another. A negative result preserves the precise conditions under which a route fails, enabling future investigations to begin with a more complete account of the problem.

The objective is a research environment in which human and artificial intelligence can engage with accumulated mathematical capacity through new, independently chosen questions.

Researcher-directed inquiry

Curiosity determines the direction.

A researcher may enter through a paper on nonlinear gravitational geometry and pursue an original question about observation, interaction or physical organization. The developing Runtime is intended to help them examine source-identified constructions, follow dependencies and investigate possible mathematical connections across disciplines.

Each qualified contribution could enrich the scientific environment available to subsequent researchers. The direction of inquiry belongs to the researcher; mathematical and empirical evidence establish the standing of its results.

This describes the intended research experience. External access and available capabilities remain subject to testing and qualification.
