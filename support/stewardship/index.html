Reporting framework · Baseline available

Scientific capacity as the measure of impact

Philanthropic participation supports the conditions through which scientific knowledge becomes more useful to future researchers. ILC’s proposed reporting framework therefore emphasizes scientific capacity established, evidence supporting it and opportunities for continuing inquiry.

The Institute intends to report progress through dated accounts of foundational research, formal infrastructure, research participation and stewardship. Existing scientific disclosures retain their standing authority. Financial and program-specific reporting will be prepared from applicable institutional records and reviewed through appropriate governance arrangements.

Proposed reporting categories

A transparent account of scientific development

Scientific discoveries

New published results and precise scientific scope; stable publication records and source editions.

Mathematical inheritance

Source-identified reusable constructions, dependency interfaces and the hypotheses needed for transfer.

Established boundaries

Obstructions, counterexamples and unresolved obligations that constrain future inquiry.

Independent examination

Scope, sources and methods of completed external examination; distinguish publication review, source review and executed formal checks.

Concordia development

Actual capabilities, dated development milestones, evidence coverage and known limits.

Research participation

Invited, active and completing participants, with cohort dates, defined denominators and consent-aware reporting.

Scientific applicability

Qualified mathematical, observational or empirical methodologies; distinguish theory, prototype and independently tested physical realization.

Financial stewardship

Reviewed contributions, expenditures, restrictions and use of funds for ILC’s charitable purposes, with period and accounting basis.

Prospective evaluation framework

How transferable scientific capability could be assessed

The central beta question is whether independent researchers can meaningfully inherit and extend the accumulated mathematical environment. These proposed indicators describe what should be recorded; they are not numerical targets or achieved outcomes.

01

Independent entry

Record invitations, onboarded researchers and active participants by cohort, with clear definitions and denominators.

02

Scientific inheritance

Document source-scoped reproductions and the time and resources needed to reach the first independently reviewed result; report only measured observations.

03

Original inquiry

Record completed investigations, including established obstructions and unresolved questions, with evidence and declared scope.

04

Cross-program transfer

Distinguish exploratory analogies from qualified mathematical bridges. Record source, target and checked compatibility conditions.

05

Continuing contribution

Track submitted, reviewed and accepted results separately. Preserve attribution, access restrictions and basis of admission.

06

Researcher experience

Collect opt-in feedback on navigation, mathematical accessibility and continuity, alongside limitations and unresolved needs.

Alpha Testing · Closed beta targeted for Q2 2027. Timing and invitations depend on continuing testing and qualification.

Reporting discipline

Preserve dates, evidence and scope

01

Dated evidence

Use a defined reporting period, dated source records, stable identifiers, editions and correction history.

02

Qualified indicators

Distinguish verified public records, source-reported claims, measured outcomes and planned activities. Record unavailable evidence explicitly.

03

Responsible disclosure

Protect participant, donor and confidential research information. Financial statements and gift-specific reports require appropriate review and approval.

04

Scientific capacity

Assess what additional capacity was established and what future inquiry it enables. Counts provide context rather than substitutes for evidence.

Annual scientific and institutional impact reports are intended as resources and reporting processes develop. The initial baseline provides a source-identified starting point. It makes no claim of audited finances, donor outcomes or completed external fellowship participation.
