What does it mean to be alive?

We explore how matter becomes organized, how living systems remain themselves through change, and how mathematics, physics, biology, and computation can make those structures visible.

Questions we are exploring

Curiosity is the organizing center.

Each research program grows around a question that can deepen, cross disciplines, and open into new questions of its own.

A discovery should remain open to the next question.

Some scientific questions unfold across years, methods, and fields. ILC gives those questions a place to keep growing through proof, computation, observation, writing, and collaboration.

Ways of exploring

We build the language the question requires.

Mathematics, formal proof, computation, physical modeling, biological data, and measurement each reveal a different aspect of the world. Our methods evolve with the problem.

01

Mathematics

Structure, relation, invariance, continuation, geometry, and the exact shape of a claim.

02

Formal proof

Machine-checkable definitions and theorems that let complex ideas become inspectable objects.

03

Computation

Experiments in representation, calibration, simulation, reconstruction, and biological organization.

04

Observation

Measurements and evidence that connect an abstract structure to the physical world.
